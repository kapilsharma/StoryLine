Née Murray. Marries Jonathan in Budapest while he is still recovering
from brain fever.

Mina is the reason the novel holds together. She learns shorthand and typewriting
"to be useful to Jonathan", and then uses those skills to collate every diary,
letter, cutting and phonograph cylinder into the single chronological narrative
that the reader is holding. She is the book's editor as well as one of its
subjects.

She is also its most active victim: attacked in chapter 21, partly turned, and
then turned into the party's chief asset — the hypnotic link to Dracula that
tells them where he is.
